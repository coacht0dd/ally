Before we arrive

Safe things you can do right now

Keep the doors shut

Every opening on a warming box costs you time. Resist the urge to keep checking inside.

Move what you can

If you have another working box with room, move the highest-value and most temperature-sensitive product first.

Write down the numbers

What the thermometer reads and at what time. Photograph any code on the controller. It speeds up the diagnosis and it is useful if an inspector asks.

Do not keep resetting it

Repeatedly cycling a unit that is locking out on a fault can turn a component failure into a bigger one.

If you smell gas, smell burning, or see anything that looks unsafe — leave the area and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

Emergency questions

Before you call

What counts as an emergency?

Any commercial refrigeration or HVAC failure where product is at risk, the business cannot operate, or a health-inspection issue is developing. For homes: no heat in freezing weather, no cooling in extreme heat, or anything that smells or sounds unsafe.

Will someone actually answer at 2am?

Yes. The number is answered by a person 24 hours a day, including nights, weekends and holidays.

How fast can you get here?

It depends on the equipment, the fault and current service availability. We will tell you honestly on the call. We do not publish an arrival-time guarantee because we would rather keep the one we give you.

What should I do right now, before anyone arrives?

For refrigeration: keep doors shut, move product to another working box if you have one, note what the thermometer reads and when, and photograph any code on the controller. For a home system: switch it off rather than let it run on a fault. If you smell gas or burning, leave and call the utility or 911 first.
